# Contract Review — Burgess Principle

Review contracts and terms of service to identify clauses where the user's rights to individual human attention may be at risk.

## When to Use

- User shares a contract, terms of service, privacy policy, or agreement for review
- User asks "what should I look out for?" before signing something
- User wants to understand whether an agreement allows automated decisions without human review
- User is entering a relationship with an institution (employer, landlord, service provider, platform) and wants to understand the power dynamics

## Procedure

1. **Read the document** — Review the full text the user provides.
2. **Flag Burgess-relevant clauses** — Identify any clause that:
   - Permits automated decision-making without human review
   - Waives the user's right to individual consideration
   - Allows data processing, profiling, or scoring without transparency
   - Grants broad enforcement powers without individual review
   - Limits the user's ability to challenge decisions or request human reconsideration
   - Requires binding arbitration that removes access to individual judicial review
3. **Explain each flagged clause** in plain language:
   - What it says (summary)
   - What it means for the user (practical impact)
   - The Burgess question: "Does this clause guarantee that a human will review your specific situation before a decision is made?"
4. **Provide recommendations**:
   - Which clauses to negotiate or push back on
   - Which clauses are standard/acceptable
   - What questions to ask the other party before signing
   - Whether legal advice is recommended for any flagged areas

## Areas to Check

| Area | What to Look For |
|------|-----------------|
| **Automated Decisions** | "We may use automated systems to..." / "algorithmic assessment" / "scoring" |
| **Data Processing** | Broad consent clauses, third-party sharing, profiling, retention periods |
| **Dispute Resolution** | Mandatory arbitration, waiver of class action, limited appeal rights |
| **Termination** | Unilateral termination without review, automated account closure |
| **Liability Limitation** | Exclusion of liability for automated errors or system failures |
| **Consent** | Blanket consent to future changes, deemed consent, opt-out vs opt-in |
| **Accessibility** | No provision for alternative formats, communication preferences, or adjustments |

## Tone Guidelines

- **Educational** — Help the user understand, not just flag
- **Balanced** — Acknowledge that some standard clauses are normal; don't alarm unnecessarily
- **Empowering** — Give the user specific questions to ask and actions to take
- **Non-legal** — This is not legal advice. State this clearly if the user needs a solicitor/lawyer

## Verification

- Every flagged clause includes a plain-language explanation
- The Burgess question is applied to relevant clauses
- Recommendations are specific and actionable
- The review explicitly states it is not legal advice
- Standard/acceptable clauses are acknowledged (not everything is a red flag)
