# Reasonable Adjustments — Burgess Principle

Help users request that institutions make reasonable adjustments for disability, sensory needs, or communication preferences — and confirm that a real person reviewed their individual circumstances.

## When to Use

- User mentions a disability, sensory need, or neurodivergent trait that affects how they interact with a service
- User has been contacted by phone when they're deaf or hard of hearing
- User has received inaccessible communications (small print, no plain-English version, no alternative formats)
- User's disability was not considered in an automated decision
- User asks about reasonable adjustments, accessibility, or the Equality Act
- User mentions autism, ADHD, dyslexia, visual impairment, hearing loss, mobility needs, or chronic illness in the context of institutional interaction

## Procedure

1. **Understand the barrier** — Ask the user: What is the institution doing (or not doing) that creates a barrier? How does their disability or need interact with the institution's process?
2. **Identify the adjustment** — Help the user articulate what would work for them. Examples:
   - Written communication instead of phone calls (hearing loss)
   - Plain-language versions of legal documents (cognitive/learning disabilities)
   - Extended deadlines for responses (chronic illness, mental health)
   - In-person appointments instead of automated processes (sensory/communication needs)
   - Alternative formats (large print, audio, Braille, easy-read)
3. **Determine jurisdiction** — Identify the applicable law:
   - **UK**: Equality Act 2010, sections 20–21 (duty to make reasonable adjustments)
   - **USA**: ADA 1990, Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act
   - **EU**: EU Accessibility Act, national disability discrimination laws
   - **Australia**: Disability Discrimination Act 1992
   - **Canada**: AODA (Ontario), Canadian Human Rights Act
4. **Draft the request** with the following structure:
   - Opening: polite identification, account/reference details
   - Disclosure: brief, factual statement of the relevant disability or need (only what the user is comfortable sharing)
   - The barrier: what the institution is doing that creates a disadvantage
   - The adjustment: what the user is asking for
   - Burgess Principle question: "I ask you to confirm that a human member of your team has personally reviewed my individual circumstances and the adjustments I am requesting, rather than applying a standard automated process."
   - Legal basis: cite the relevant law
   - Response deadline: 14–28 days

## Tone Guidelines

- **Warm and respectful** — The user is sharing personal information; honour that
- **Matter-of-fact** — Disability is not a weakness; adjustments are a right, not a favour
- **Specific** — Name the exact barrier and the exact adjustment
- **Never patronising** — The user knows their needs; help them articulate them clearly

## Verification

- The request names a specific barrier and a specific adjustment
- The Burgess Principle question is included
- The correct legal framework is cited for the user's jurisdiction
- The user's disclosure is minimal and respectful (only what they chose to share)
- The tone is warm, clear, and rights-based throughout
